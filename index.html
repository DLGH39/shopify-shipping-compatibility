What Shopify is changing

Shopify calls the underlying developer transition market-driven shipping. Merchant-facing documentation uses shipping options by market. The practical change is that merchant-owned shipping configuration moves toward Markets → Shipping rather than the legacy shipping-profile model.

Shopify says merchant rollout begins October 1, 2026. Its developer guidance also says merchants using affected apps are not automatically moved until Shopify confirms compatibility, while manual opt-in before an app is upgraded can cause breakage.

Why this matters if you use a shipping app

Missing rates
A checkout can fail to show the shipping services you expect.
Configuration mismatch
An app may still assume the legacy shipping-profile structure.
Premature migration
Opting in before your app is ready can create avoidable operational risk.

One configuration trap we verified

Easyship's current public Shopify Rates at Checkout guide still describes setup through Shipping and Delivery → General. In our market-driven test store, the working path was Markets → United States → Shipping → Carrier or app calculated.

This does not mean Easyship's documentation is wrong everywhere. It means some current setup guidance still reflects Shopify's legacy workflow while the new shipping model is centered in Markets.

What PASS, FAIL, and UNKNOWN mean here

  • PASS — Shopify explicitly confirms compatibility, the vendor explicitly confirms it, or a controlled Shopify test proves the relevant workflow works.
  • FAIL — direct platform or controlled-test evidence shows the relevant workflow is incompatible.
  • UNKNOWN — available evidence does not satisfy either threshold. We do not infer compatibility from App Store availability, ordinary Shopify integration, or the absence of complaints.

Our test method

We used a synthetic Shopify development store with the market-driven-shipping feature preview enabled, a physical control product, inventory, package dimensions, and known native shipping rates. Native checkout was verified first. Third-party apps were then evaluated against the same environment.

For Easyship, the final checkout showed Easyship-calculated services beside the native Shopify Standard and Express controls. For Intuitive Shipping, we stopped at Shopify's explicit incompatibility warning rather than installing through it. ShipStation remains deliberately unclassified beyond UNKNOWN.

Before you change anything

App compatibility can change quickly during a platform rollout. Recheck your app's current Shopify/vendor guidance and test your own rate logic before opting in. A PASS here proves the workflow we tested, not that one app is a functional replacement for another merchant's full rule set.
